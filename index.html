About

Researching intelligent systems that work with people

From Sep 2026 I will join Computer Science and Software Engineering at The University of Western Australia.

My research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, brain–computer interfaces and robotics. I develop computational methods that learn from neural, physiological, behavioural and contextual signals to infer human state and intent, enabling more adaptive and reliable interaction between people and intelligent systems.

A recurring theme across my work is translation from algorithms to real systems. I combine brain and physiological information processing, multimodal and deep learning, wearable sensing, human–robot interaction and adaptive autonomy with experimental validation and system integration. The aim is not only to improve model performance, but to build intelligent technologies that remain useful across people, tasks and real-world environments.

Research

Research themes

My work brings together sensing, learning and autonomous decision-making to create systems that can better understand people and collaborate with them. Three broad themes connect my current and future research.

01

Brain–Computer Interfaces

I develop non-invasive approaches for extracting useful information from brain and physiological signals, with particular interest in robust brain information processing, wearable sensing and practical brain–computer interaction.

  • Brain information
  • BCI
  • Wearable sensing
02

Human-centred & Multimodal AI

I investigate AI models that learn from multiple sources of human and contextual information, aiming for systems that are personalised, adaptive, data-efficient and robust to the variability found in real-world human data.

  • Multimodal learning
  • Deep learning
  • Human-centred AI
03

Human–Autonomy & Intelligent Robotics

I study how humans and autonomous systems can share information, decisions and control. This includes human–robot teaming, adaptive autonomy, cognitive-aware interaction and intelligent systems designed for complex operational environments.

  • Robotics
  • Shared autonomy
  • Human–AI teaming
Research trajectory

From human signals to deployable intelligence

My research follows a consistent pathway: understand informative signals from people, learn robust representations, turn those representations into adaptive interaction, and validate the resulting systems beyond controlled laboratory conditions.

Human signals

Neural, physiological, behavioural and contextual sensing.

Learning & representation

Signal processing, multimodal learning and robust AI models.

Adaptive intelligence

Inference of human state and intent to support interaction and decision-making.

Integrated systems

BCI, human–AI and robotic systems evaluated in realistic settings.

GeneralisationMethods that remain reliable across people, sessions, tasks and environments.
Human-centred evaluationPerformance measured together with usability, trust, workload and interaction quality.
TranslationResearch designed with deployment constraints and real-world integration in mind.
Research highlight

Brain–Robot Interaction: from signals to integrated systems

A long-running Defence research program brought together brain–computer interfaces, AI, augmented-reality interfaces and robotic platforms. My role focused on technical integration and translation across sensing, algorithms, software and robotics, culminating in multi-user and multi-robot demonstrations.

The project illustrates a recurring focus in my research: moving beyond offline algorithms to build and evaluate complete human–AI systems in realistic operational settings.
